Security Resources

A few plain-language reminders you can use today.

Spotting phishing emails

Phishing emails try to trick us into giving away information. We'll show you how to spot them at our seminars, but here are a few quick signs to watch for:

  • An urgent tone that pressures you to act immediately
  • Requests for passwords, account numbers, or gift cards
  • Links or email addresses that look slightly "off" from the real company
  • Unexpected attachments you weren't expecting

Good habit: If an email or call feels urgent or unusual, take a breath and contact the company directly using a phone number or website you already know — not one from the message itself.

Strong passwords, simply explained

Strong passwords help protect your account. Let's make sure yours is secure — a good password is long (12+ characters), unique to each account, and easy for you to remember but hard for others to guess. A password manager can do the remembering for you.

Watch out: Be cautious of any message asking you to "verify your account" by entering your password through a link. When in doubt, log in directly through the company's app or website instead.

Common scams targeting seniors

Scammers often pose as tech support, government agencies, or even family members in distress. They rely on urgency and fear to keep you from thinking it through.

If someone asks you to pay with gift cards, wire transfers, or cryptocurrency, it is almost always a scam — no legitimate organization asks for payment this way.
